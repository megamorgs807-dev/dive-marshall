# Bude's own club dives elsewhere, not here

The single most important fact for building an accurate Bude Dive Club website is also the most inconvenient one: **Bude Sub Aqua Dive Club's own FAQ states that routine day-trip diving happens on the South Devon and Dorset coast, not locally off north Cornwall** — "Longer weekend trips tend to venture further afield to Cornwall and South/South West Wales" ([Bude Dive Club FAQ](https://www.budediveclub.co.uk/faq/)). Beyond that admission, verifiable, dive-specific detail for the Hartland Point–Crackington Haven stretch is thin. There is no published list of named local dive sites with depths and visibility, no confirmed charted wreck with a usable scuba depth on this exact stretch, and no diver-sighting record (Seasearch, BSAC newsletter, or otherwise) tying specific marine life to a specific spot near Bude. What does exist, and exists richly, is: the club's real organisational facts (founding, boat, compressor, membership); one real local outing (Millook, via a single YouTube video); a formally protected mainland habitat (the Hartland Point to Tintagel Marine Conservation Zone, with named protected species); well-documented historical strandings along the "Shipwreck Coast"; extensively documented wrecks, seals, and ecology at **Lundy Island**, reached from Ilfracombe rather than Bude; and a consistent, well-sourced picture of why this coast is hard to dive casually — Bristol Channel tides among the largest in the world, constant Atlantic groundswell, and RNLI-documented rip currents at several named beaches. A website built on these verified facts, honestly framed, will be more credible — and more useful to a real diver — than one populated with invented site names or imagined trip reports.

## The club's real profile is well documented — use it as-is

Unlike almost everything else in this research, Bude Sub Aqua Dive Club's own website was successfully retrieved in full (after bypassing an expired SSL certificate), so its organisational facts are first-party and high-confidence. The club is the **715th BSAC branch**, **formed in 1975**, with members "aged from 14–75" drawn from "Bude, Holsworthy, Launceston, Hartland, Camelford and other surrounding areas" ([About Bude Dive Club](https://www.budediveclub.co.uk/about-us/); [Home](https://www.budediveclub.co.uk/)). Winter training is held in **Holsworthy Pool** on Sunday afternoons, typically in March/April ([Home](https://www.budediveclub.co.uk/)). The club's FAQ describes the standard club dive as "a mixture of Reef/Wreck to a maximum of 50m," and explicitly notes it is **not bound to one area** because it owns its own boat ([FAQ](https://www.budediveclub.co.uk/faq/)). That boat is a **Humber 5.5m RIB with a 90hp outboard**, fitted with GPS, a sonar echo-sounder, VHF and flares; the club also owns its own 230-bar air compressor, which it describes as "the only one in the area," with the nearest alternatives at Plymouth, Exeter or Newquay ([Equipment](https://www.budediveclub.co.uk/equipment/)). The club's published "Latest Trips" list, however, runs out in 2017 and is dominated by warm-water holiday diving abroad — Sardinia, Croatia, the Red Sea, Corfu, Malta, Lanzarote, Hurghada, Turkey, Mallorca — with several years marked "no report," strongly suggesting the trip-report feature has been dormant for roughly a decade ([Home](https://www.budediveclub.co.uk/)). The one piece of marketing copy that evokes the local coast at all — "when you experience the exhilaration of diving off of North Cornwall's spectacular coastline" — names no actual site ([Equipment](https://www.budediveclub.co.uk/equipment/)). The club lists the **Ilfracombe and North Devon Sub-Aqua Club** as its only named "Local BSAC Club" affiliation ([Diving Organisation Links](https://www.budediveclub.co.uk/diving-organisation-links/)), and that neighbouring club is worth knowing about precisely because it does the local-site documentation Bude's own site doesn't: it publishes named, graded sites such as **The George Lamb** (12–20m, Eastaway Rock) and **The George Lockett** (steamship lost 10 February 1982, 15–20m), plus drift dives at **Penns Port to Bennets Mouth** and **Lee Bay**, while diving "the Bristol Channel, Lundy Island, the Cornish, South Devon and Dorset coasts" roughly April–October ([Ilfracombe and North Devon Sub-Aqua Club](https://www.ilfracombediveclub.com/our-club/dive-sites/)). A website for Bude Dive Club should describe the club honestly on these terms — a small, boat- and compressor-owning BSAC branch with a long history and a genuine local identity, whose signature trips have historically been further afield — rather than inventing a roster of local "signature dives" the club's own records don't support.

## Named sites near Bude exist, but as a thin, specific list — not a catalogue

Resist the temptation to build out a long "dive site directory" for the Bude stretch itself: the real list is short. The clearest citable description is that "Bude...offers easy access to many sheltered dive sites, around **Dizzard Point** and the **Cambeak** area," with "plenty of shallow and deep wreck sites" marked on **Admiralty Chart 1178** ([Visit Cornwall TV](https://www.visitcornwall.tv/cornwall-divers-guide-north-coast/)). Cambeak is a genuine named headland immediately south of Crackington Haven, confirmed independently via coast-path walk descriptions ([Visorando](https://www.visorando.com/en/walk-cambeak-and-east-wood-from-crackington-h/)), so this functions as a descriptive diving ground spanning a few miles of reef rather than one single labelled site. **Crackington Haven** itself is a known shore-entry beach, and **Millook**, a cove between Widemouth Bay and Dizzard Point, is the one location with direct video evidence of a Bude Sub Aqua Dive Club outing — a July 2017 YouTube upload titled "Scuba Diving off Millock [Millook]...with Bude Dive Club" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wK7MEARBF8Y)) — making Millook the single most defensible "the club actually dives here" claim this research turned up. Widemouth Bay has two named orientation features, **Black Rock** at the bay's southern end and **Camel Rock** at the northern end, both reef patches with rich rock-pool life, though these are surf/snorkel landmarks rather than confirmed scuba sites. By contrast, **Sandymouth, Northcott Mouth, Duckpool and Crooklets** should not appear on a dive-sites page at all: multiple non-diving safety sources explicitly describe currents there as dangerous at all states of the tide, and no diving source names a dive site at any of them. Depth data for this stretch is similarly sparse: the only concrete figure found is that **25m (82ft) depth is generally available only about half a mile offshore** near Bude ([Visit Cornwall TV](https://www.visitcornwall.tv/cornwall-divers-guide-north-coast/)); no visibility-in-metres figure specific to Bude, Widemouth Bay, or Crackington Haven exists anywhere in the sources gathered — only a generic Cornwall-wide "up to 10m in good conditions" estimate that should be labelled as an estimate, not a site fact, if used at all. A website can honestly say "we dive the sheltered coves around Dizzard Point, Cambeak, Crackington Haven and Millook, conditions permitting" — it should not claim named, graded dive trails the research couldn't verify.

## Wrecks are a real historical draw, but mostly not scuba-diveable here

The "Shipwreck Coast" framing is true and well evidenced, but the realistic picture is nuanced: this stretch has an estimated **1,000 recorded wrecks within five miles of shore** ([Divernet](https://divernet.com/scuba-diving/cornwall-is-one-of-the-most-popular-diving-spots-in-the-uk/); [Visit Cornwall TV](https://www.visitcornwall.tv/cornwall-divers-guide-north-coast/)), yet almost all the individually named wrecks between Hartland Point and Boscastle are **historical shoreline strandings**, not surveyed offshore scuba sites with a known depth. The clearest exception with confirmed, visible remains is the German E-boat **S89**, which broke up at **Tremoutha Haven**, about half a mile south of Crackington Haven, on 5 October 1946; her three Daimler-Benz engines, prop shaft and gearbox are still visible among the boulders at low tide — but this is an intertidal site, not a scuba depth dive ([Submerged.co.uk](https://www.submerged.co.uk/crackington-haven-s89/)). Other named historical wrecks on this coast carry real stories but no diveable depth in the record: the **SS Bencoolen** (1862, Summerleaze Beach, Bude, 27 of 33 crew drowned, figurehead preserved in Bude's town museum), the brig **Caledonia** (1842, Sharpnose Point, crew buried at Morwenstow beneath the ship's figurehead), the **Jessie Logan** (1843, below Willapark near Boscastle), and a cluster near Hartland Point/Hartland Quay — the modern **MS Johanna** (1982, grounded within 400m of Hartland Point lighthouse, decaying hull still visible), the **SS Rosalia** (1904, Hartland Quay, "iron bones...seen at low tide"), and the **SS Hoche** (1882) — are all shoreline strandings rather than offshore dives (see the summary table below). None of these carries a confirmed scuba depth in metres in the available record, and a wreck database search (Wrecksite.eu, UKHO) to establish precise positions and depths for this specific stretch was not achievable with the tools used in this research — that remains an open task for a human with direct database access.

By contrast, **Lundy Island** — a realistic special-trip destination for a Bude club with its own boat, though normally reached via charter from **Ilfracombe**, not Bude directly — has a genuinely rich, depth-confirmed, named wreck roster, including two legally **Protected Wrecks**: the Confederate blockade-runner paddle steamer **PS Iona II** (sank 1864, ~25m, boilers and funnels visible, licence required to dive) and the pre-dreadnought battleship **HMS Montagu** (wrecked on Shutter Rock, 1906, 4–15m, Scheduled Monument since 2019) ([BSAC — Diving the Iona II](https://www.bsac.com/news-and-blog/diving-the-iona-ii-paddle-steamer-off-lundy-island/); [Submerged.co.uk — HMS Montagu](https://submerged.co.uk/hms-montagu-lundy-island/)). Also diveable at Lundy: the intact coaster **MV Robert** (sank 1975, 24m, "the only intact wreck to dive at Lundy") and the **Carmine Filomena** (1937, 8–18m, now kelp-covered) ([Lundy Diving](https://www.lundydiving.co.uk/dive)). If the website wants to offer members a genuine "bucket-list" wreck trip, Lundy — explicitly flagged as needing advance planning for Protection of Wrecks Act licences on Iona II/Montagu, and an 8-hour round trip from Ilfracombe — is the honest answer, not an invented wreck off Bude itself.

## Marine life claims should lean on the MCZ, not on Bude-specific sightings

The strongest, most citable marine-life fact for the mainland coast is a formal government designation: the **Hartland Point to Tintagel Marine Conservation Zone** covers this entire stretch (including Bude, Widemouth and Crackington Haven) and specifically protects **pink sea-fan coral** (*Eunicella verrucosa*) colonies on circalittoral rock, **honeycomb worm reefs** (*Sabellaria alveolata* — intertidal structures that can extend "tens of metres across and up to a metre tall"), and "fragile sponge & anthozoan communities" mixing sponges, anemones and sea-fan corals on deeper rock ([Defra, Hartland Point to Tintagel MCZ Factsheet](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a80d86e40f0b62305b8d796/mcz-hartland-point-tintagel-factsheet.pdf)). This is genuinely site-specific and can be used with confidence. What cannot be used with confidence is any claim of **divers at Bude itself** encountering seals, dolphins, or basking sharks — the only source for that is general Bude tourism copy ("keep an eye out for seals and the occasional dolphin and basking shark") rather than a diver report or survey ([Visit Bude](https://www.visitbude.info/wildlife-in-bude/)), and Cornwall Wildlife Trust's own 2024 Seasearch annual report — the most current organised volunteer-diver survey for the county — **does not mention Bude, Hartland Point, Widemouth Bay, or Crackington Haven anywhere**, with all its 2024 highlights located on Cornwall's south coast ([Seasearch Cornwall 2024](https://www.cornwallwildlifetrust.org.uk/sites/default/files/2025-08/Seasearch%20Cornwall%20annual%20report%202024.pdf)). Fish species for the mainland stretch (wrasse, pollack, conger, bull huss, dogfish, bass) are well evidenced only via angling reports at Hartland Point and Millook, not diver sightings, and should be labelled as general reef-ground inference rather than confirmed underwater reports ([North Devon Angling News](https://www.northdevonanglingnews.co.uk/tag/clovelly/)). **Lundy, again, is where the real, vivid marine-life material lives**: a resident colony of roughly 200 grey seals that "enjoy interacting with divers, snorkelers and swimmers" and will "often initiate play," formally managed with named haul-out exclusion zones at Brazen Ward, Three-quarter Wall Bay and Surf Point ([Diving Lundy Information Sheet](https://www.landmarktrust.org.uk/globalassets/1-aa-new-responsive-site-images/website/lundy/discover-lundy/activities-and-things-to-do/diving/diving-lundy-information-sheet_2024.pdf)); a scientifically documented No-Take Zone where landable lobsters became **427% more abundant** than in fished control areas within four years of protection ([Hoskin et al. 2009](https://www.landmarktrust.org.uk/globalassets/1-aa-new-responsive-site-images/website/lundy/discover-lundy/conservation/lundy-ntz.pdf)); and a kelp forest notable for an expanding population of warm-water "golden kelp" (*Laminaria ochroleuca*) at its documented northern range limit ([Smale et al.](https://wernberglab.org/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/2015_smale-et-al-ma-ecol-lam-ochroleuca.pdf)). A website that wants vivid, true marine-life storytelling should draw it from Lundy trips and the formal MCZ designation, and keep Bude-specific wildlife claims generic and hedged.

## Conditions are genuinely demanding — and that's a feature, not a flaw, to state plainly

Three converging, well-sourced facts explain why this coast is hard to dive casually and why the club's own day trips go elsewhere. First, **tidal range**: Bude sits at the mouth of the Bristol Channel, which has the second-highest tidal range in the world; spring tides at Bude itself reach roughly **8 metres**, against a neap range of only about 3 metres ([Tide-Forecast: Bude](https://www.tide-forecast.com/locations/Bude/tides/latest)), compressing currents into short, fast-moving slack-water windows. Second, **exposure**: this is a fully open, Atlantic-facing coast with no offshore shelter — unlike Falmouth, the Lizard or Scilly on the south coast — so it "suits divers with a boat operator who knows local conditions, rather than casual shore diving" ([Cornwall Online](https://www.cornwall-online.co.uk/waterside/diving.htm)), and Hartland Point/Hartland Quay specifically are noted for "some of the roughest seas in winter" ([Wikipedia — Hartland Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartland_Point)). Third, and most important for a safety-conscious club website, **named hazard documentation is real and specific**: Bude RNLI lifeguards have explicitly warned that **Duckpool** is unpatrolled and poses "significant risks due to strong rip currents," and it "tragically claimed a life in 2023" ([CornishStuff](https://cornishstuff.com/bude/bude-rnli-lifeguards-warn-against-swimming-at-duckpool-beach/)); **Northcott Mouth** has "some of the strongest currents on the North Cornwall coast, with a vicious cross rip," and "on big highs the beach all but disappears" with sections cut off beneath the cliffs ([Cornwall Beach Guide](https://www.cornwallbeachguide.co.uk/northcornwall/northcott/northcott.htm)); and even **Summerleaze**, Bude's own town beach, saw RNLI lifeguards rescue three swimmers from a rip current on neap tides in August 2025, showing danger here isn't limited to spring tides ([RNLI](https://rnli.org/news-and-media/2025/august/06/rnli-lifeguards-rescue-three-swimmers-at-summerleaze-beach-in-bude)). Water temperature follows a typical UK Atlantic pattern — roughly **7–9°C in late winter/spring, peaking around 17°C in August** ([Seatemperature.org](https://www.seatemperature.org/europe/united-kingdom/bude-average.htm)) — and the practical diving season, as elsewhere in Cornwall, runs roughly **May to September**, though no source gave a season specific to Bude rather than Cornwall generally, and this coast's exposure likely narrows that window further than at a sheltered south-coast site. None of these hazard reports is diving-specific — all are swimmer/surfer incidents — but the underlying physical hazard (strong rip currents, tide-dependent access, fast-moving currents at submerged rock) transfers directly to dive planning, and a club website should present it that way rather than glossing over it.

## What this means for building the website

The accurate version of this story is more interesting than a fabricated one: Bude Sub Aqua Dive Club is a genuine, half-century-old BSAC branch with its own boat and compressor, whose honest local identity is a handful of real coves (Dizzard Point, Cambeak, Crackington Haven, Millook) dived when Atlantic conditions allow, balanced against a club culture that — by its own published FAQ — takes its routine trips to calmer South Devon and Dorset waters and treats Lundy, reached via Ilfracombe, and overseas liveaboards as its bigger-ticket diving. The honest framing for marine life and "wow factor" content is the formally protected Hartland Point to Tintagel MCZ (pink sea fans, honeycomb worm reefs) for the mainland, and Lundy's scientifically documented seals, Protected Wrecks and lobster recovery for anything more dramatic. The biggest remaining research gap — and the clearest next step before publishing a site that claims more specificity than this — is that the club's own dive-sites page, trip-report archive, and any Facebook/Instagram presence post-2017 were not independently retrievable in this pass; a direct conversation with the club (or a visit to budediveclub.co.uk once its certificate is fixed) would likely surface real, current site names and recent trip reports that no amount of further web research can substitute for.
