# North Cornwall Coast Diving: Bude to Hartland Point / Crackington Haven

## What are the actual named dive sites divers use along this coast?

### Takeaway
Hard, specific named-site data is thin across the public web. The clearest named references are **Dizzard Point** and the **Cambeak** area (both near Crackington Haven) as a general "sheltered dive sites" zone, plus **Crackington Haven** beach itself as a known shore-entry spot. Several of the other requested locations (Sandymouth, Northcott Mouth, Duckpool) are explicitly described by non-diving sources as unsuitable for diving/snorkelling due to dangerous currents, and no dive-specific source names a site there. No dedicated North Cornwall "dive site guide" (comparable to divelists for Falmouth, Plymouth, or Swanage) was found.

### Cited Findings
- "Bude, in particular, offers easy access to many sheltered dive sites, around Dizzard Point and the Cambeak area. There are plenty of shallow and deep wreck sites here, which can be found marked on Admiralty chart No. 1178." — [Visit Cornwall TV, Cornwall Divers Guide North Coast](https://www.visitcornwall.tv/cornwall-divers-guide-north-coast/)
- Cambeak is a named headland immediately south of Crackington Haven (reached by the coast path walk "Cambeak and East Wood from Crackington Haven"), confirming it as a geographic feature divers reference, not a generic description — [Visorando walk description](https://www.visorando.com/en/walk-cambeak-and-east-wood-from-crackington-h/); [cornwalls.co.uk photo page "Cambeak - Crackington Haven"](https://www.cornwalls.co.uk/photos/cambeak-crackington-haven-2529.htm)
- Crackington Haven is accessed via a walk down the beach for shore diving, with the dive following sand and rock formations toward Pencannow Point (the headland north of the bay); divers there have reported shoals of bass and grey seals — [search-aggregated finding, see Gaps below for primary-source caveat]
- "The stretch running from Bude down through Tintagel, Padstow and Newquay to Perranporth…rewards divers with dramatic cliffs, sea caves and an estimated 1,000 recorded wrecks within five miles of the shore" — but this article names no specific site in the Bude/Hartland stretch; it directs divers toward Newquay-area sites (Poltexas Reef, Newquay Headland, Porthminster Reef) which are outside this project's scope — [Divernet](https://divernet.com/scuba-diving/cornwall-is-one-of-the-most-popular-diving-spots-in-the-uk/)
- Widemouth Bay has two named reef/rock features that divers/snorkellers could reference for orientation: **Black Rock** (large reef patch, southern end of the bay, extensive rock pools, rich marine life) and **Camel Rock** (reef patch at the northern end) — [cornwall-based beach/surf guides, aggregated]
- No source found names any specific dive site at Sandymouth, Northcott Mouth, Duckpool, or Crooklets. Multiple non-diving sources explicitly state Sandymouth/Duckpool currents are "dangerous at all states of the tide" and unsuitable for swimming (implying unsuitable for diving too), and that Northcott Mouth "is not a beach for snorkelling or diving."
- Hartland Quay Beach is listed by a beach-guide tourism source as offering "diving & snorkelling" among its activities "on a calm day," alongside bathing, surfing and fishing, but no named dive site, depth, or visibility detail accompanies this — [The Beach Guide, Hartland Quay Beach](https://www.thebeachguide.co.uk/south-west-england/devon/hartland-quay.htm)

### Inferences
- The lack of named sites at Sandymouth/Northcott Mouth/Duckpool/Crooklets is very likely because these beaches face full Atlantic swell with strong rip/longshore currents, making them primarily surf beaches rather than dive locations — divers in this area appear to favour the more sheltered coves (Crackington Haven, the Cambeak/Dizzard Point stretch) instead.
- "Dizzard Point and the Cambeak area" most likely functions as a descriptive diving ground (a few miles of coast with multiple informal/boat-accessed sites) rather than one single named site — consistent with how Admiralty chart 1178 would show scattered reef/wreck marks rather than one labelled dive site.

## What depths and visibility ranges are reported at each?

### Takeaway
Only one concrete regional depth figure was found, and no visibility figures at all for this specific stretch of coast; visibility data that does exist in search results is for other parts of Cornwall (Newquay/St Ives area) and should not be assumed to apply to Bude/Hartland.

### Cited Findings
- Near Bude, "82ft (25m) [depth] generally available only 0.5 miles offshore" — [Visit Cornwall TV, Cornwall Divers Guide North Coast](https://www.visitcornwall.tv/cornwall-divers-guide-north-coast/)
- Crackington Haven shore dive reportedly starts in shallow water "around two meters" at entry before divers follow sand/rock out toward Pencannow Point (depth increasing further out is implied but not quantified) — unverified primary source, see Gap above
- General Cornwall-wide (not site-specific) context: "the best season for scuba diving in Cornwall runs from May to September, when water temperatures rise to 14–18°C and visibility is at its peak" — [search-aggregated, PADI/Cornwall diving overview pages]
- For comparison only (NOT this coast — Padstow/Harlyn Bay, further south/west and outside scope): Harlyn Bay reaches "maximum depths of around 14 metres at high tide" — included only to show the kind of site-specific figure that exists for nearby Cornish coast but was not found for Bude/Hartland sites — [search-aggregated]

### Inferences
- Given the 25m-at-half-a-mile-offshore figure, inshore/shore-entry sites (Crackington Haven, Widemouth Bay reefs) are likely shallow — plausibly single-digit to low-teens metres — while boat dives further off Dizzard Point/Cambeak reach into the 20m+ range. This is an inference, not a directly sourced depth-by-site breakdown.

### Gaps
- No visibility (metres of underwater viz) figures specific to Bude, Widemouth Bay, Crackington Haven, Dizzard Point, Cambeak, Sharpnose Point, or Hartland were found anywhere in this research. This is a significant gap — visibility on this north-facing, swell-exposed, partly sand/silt coast is reported anecdotally elsewhere (not sourced here) to be changeable and often reduced after swell/rain, but no citable figure exists in the sources gathered.
- No depth figures found for Widemouth Bay, Sharpnose Point/Morwenstow, or Hartland Point/Hartland Quay waters specifically.

## What are the entry/exit points and access logistics (car parks, slipways, tidal access windows)?

### Takeaway
Only Crackington Haven has any entry-point description in the sources found (walk down the beach, start shallow), and even that is unverified-primary-source (see Gap above). No slipway, car park, or tide-timing detail for diving specifically was found for any of the nine named locations in the brief.

### Cited Findings
- Crackington Haven: "stony foreshore but a sandy beach is revealed at low water," toilet facilities near the beach, lifeguard cover in summer; diving access described (in the unverified summary) as a walk down the beach with shallow entry — [general Crackington Haven beach-guide sources, e.g. Visit Cornwall beach page]
- Lower Sharpnose Point (climbing, not diving, context) "is only accessible 3 hours either side of low tide, as the tide comes in very quickly and can catch people unexpectedly" — included as a strong proxy warning for anyone considering water access near Sharpnose Point, even though it is sourced from a climbing guide, not a diving one — [thecrag.com, Lower Sharpnose Point](https://www.thecrag.com/en/climbing/united-kingdom/lower-sharpnose-point)
- Hartland Point has a tide race near the lighthouse on the ebb (roughly 1.5 knots, building over about two miles down from Hartland Quay), classed by a sea-kayaking tidal-hazards resource as a "medium risk" tidal feature, worst in wind-against-tide or swell conditions, calmest near slack water — relevant to any diver/boat considering that headland — [Goosejuice tidal races resource](https://www.goosejuice.co.uk/resources/tidal-races-around-britain/); [The Lone Kayaker, "Hairy Hartland"](https://thelonekayaker.wordpress.com/2019/09/22/hairy-hartland/)
- Sandymouth and Duckpool: currents are described by tourism/safety sources as "dangerous at all states of the tide," which would affect any dive entry/exit planning there even though no diver source discusses it directly — [aggregated beach-safety sources]

### Inferences
- The tide-sensitivity called out at Sharpnose Point and Hartland Point for climbers/kayakers almost certainly applies with equal or greater force to any diver entry/exit at those same points — strong tidal currents and fast-covering rock platforms are a consistent theme up and down this specific stretch of coast.
- The absence of any described slipway anywhere in the sources (contrast with, e.g., well-documented slipways at Falmouth or Looe) suggests shore diving on this stretch is genuinely beach/rock-scramble entry only, with no formal harbour or slipway infrastructure between Bude and Hartland Quay — but this is inferred from absence of evidence, not confirmed.

### Gaps
- No car park names/locations tied to diving access were found for any of the nine locations.
- No specific tidal access "window" (e.g., "dive 2 hours either side of high water") was found for any named dive site on this coast — only the general Sharpnose/Hartland tidal-hazard information above, which is not diving-specific.
- No information found on whether any of these sites have ever had a slipway, harbour, or formal boat-launch point used by divers (Bude itself has a harbour/canal and lifeboat station, but no source connects this to dive-boat launching).

## Is there any mention of Bude Sub Aqua Dive Club's own site list or trip reports naming specific sites?

### Takeaway
The club's existence and basic profile is confirmed, but its own site content (site lists, trip reports) could not be retrieved in this session, and no third-party source quotes specific named sites from Bude Sub Aqua Dive Club's trip reports beyond one informal YouTube video title.

### Cited Findings
- Bude Sub Aqua Dive Club was formed in 1975, is BSAC-instructor-led, has members aged 14–75, and dives "off North Cornwall's coastline," offering recreational diving and snorkelling — [Bude Sub Aqua Dive Club, budediveclub.co.uk (via search snippet only — direct fetch failed, see Gaps)]
- A YouTube video titled "Scuba Diving off Millock 7 July 2017 with Bude Dive Club" confirms the club has dived at **Millook** (also spelled "Millock"), a cove just south of Widemouth Bay between Widemouth and Dizzard Point — this is the one piece of evidence tying the club to a specific named location beyond the generic "Dizzard Point/Cambeak" description — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wK7MEARBF8Y)
- An older print-era guide (reproduced in a general Cornwall diving/wreck-and-reef page) notes divers can contact "the local BS-AC club at the Pool & Leisure Centre (01288-353714)" for Bude-area diving information — consistent with Bude Sub Aqua Dive Club historically being based at/near Bude's Pool & Leisure Centre, and a possible lead for a human to call directly — [Cornwall Online, Diving in Cornwall — Wreck & Reef Guide](https://www.cornwall-online.co.uk/waterside/diving.htm)

### Inferences
- Millook is very likely a regularly-used club site given a dedicated trip video exists for it, and it fits the geographic pattern (sheltered cove between Widemouth Bay and Dizzard Point/Cambeak) suggested by the Visit Cornwall TV description.

### Gaps
- The club's own website (budediveclub.co.uk) could not be loaded in this session — direct fetch failed with an "certificate has expired" TLS error, and the Wayback Machine (web.archive.org) is not reachable from this research tool. **This is the single highest-value unresolved lead**: a human visiting the site directly in an ordinary browser (which may tolerate or bypass the cert warning, or the cert may since have been renewed) could very likely find an actual named dive-site list, launch/slipway info, and trip reports. The phone number 01288-353714 (Bude Pool & Leisure Centre, per an older published guide) may also be a way to reach the club directly, though this number should be treated as potentially outdated.
- No BSAC branch newsletter content, Facebook group posts, or additional trip-report text naming specific sites (depths, visibility, what was seen) could be retrieved beyond the single Millook video title.
