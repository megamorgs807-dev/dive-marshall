# Shipwrecks: Hartland Point to Crackington Haven/Boscastle, and Lundy Island

## What named wrecks are actually dived or documented along this specific stretch of coast (Hartland Point to Crackington Haven/Boscastle)?

### Takeaway
This stretch of coast (the "Shipwreck Coast") has a very long documented history of strandings/wrecks (hundreds of vessels over centuries), but the number of wrecks with confirmed, citable **dive-specific** detail (precise GPS position, charted depth, confirmed diveable remains) is small. Most historically-named wrecks here are shore strandings now reduced to scattered iron/timber visible at low tide rather than offshore dive sites with real depth — the one clear exception is the German E-boat S89 at Tremoutha Haven (intertidal, not a scuba dive) and the general statement that ~1,000 wrecks lie within 5 miles of this coast per local dive-guide sources, without a name-by-name breakdown.

### Cited Findings
- The German Schnell-boot (E-boat) **S89** ran ashore and broke up at **Tremoutha Haven, roughly half a mile from Crackington Haven**, between Bude and Boscastle, on 5 October 1946, after breaking free of her tow in heavy weather while being taken to Wales as a target vessel; her three Daimler-Benz engines, prop shaft, and gearbox assemblies are still visible among the boulders at low tide. — [Submerged.co.uk](https://www.submerged.co.uk/crackington-haven-s89/)
- A general north-Cornwall diving guide states depths of "82ft (25m), generally available only 0.5 miles offshore," and that "1,000 recorded shipwrecks" lie offshore within 130ft (40m) or 5 miles of this coast, with wreck sites "marked on Admiralty chart No. 1178" (Approaches to the Bristol Channel); the guide names launch points at Bude, and dive areas around **Dizzard Point and Cambeak**, and "rocky coves near Tintagel and Boscastle" — but does not itemise individual wreck names/positions for the Bude–Boscastle stretch specifically. — [Visit Cornwall TV — Cornwall Divers Guide North Coast](https://www.visitcornwall.tv/cornwall-divers-guide-north-coast/)
- The same source separately lists named wrecks (S.S. Escurial near Portreath; Maria Assumpta, 1995, near Pentire Point; M.V. Castor 1, 1980, at Port Isaac harbour entrance; Skopelos Sky, Greek freighter, Hell Bay near Port Isaac) — but these all lie well south of Boscastle, closer to Padstow/Port Isaac, and are **outside the Hartland Point–Boscastle stretch** this research is scoped to. — [Visit Cornwall TV — Cornwall Divers Guide North Coast](https://www.visitcornwall.tv/cornwall-divers-guide-north-coast/)
- The **Jessie Logan**, an American-built English cargo ship, struck rocks and wrecked below Willapark near Boscastle in January 1843, carrying buffalo horns and hides, cotton, shellac, raw sugar, flax, rice, and dyewood (the dyewood "coloured the water red for a considerable distance"); all crew escaped safely. — [The Riverside, Boscastle](https://riversideboscastle.co.uk/boscastle-a-history-of-shipwrecks-and-wrecking/); also [Jessie Logan (ship) — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessie_Logan_(ship))
- The French brigantine **Gazelle**, laden with coal, was "totally wrecked" at Boscastle in a gale in 1899; two of her four-man crew drowned. — [uboatproject.wales](https://uboatproject.wales/2018/04/24/100-mlynedd-yn-ol-suddwyd-yr-agerlong-yr-ss-boscastle-ar-7-ebrill-1918-gan-yr-u-111/) (cross-referenced via search snippet)
- The steamship **SS Boscastle** was torpedoed and sunk by the German U-boat **U-111** on 7 April 1918 while carrying Welsh steam coal from Barry to the British Fleet at Scapa Flow; she had been chartered by the Admiralty for war work. — [uboat.net — Steamer Boscastle](https://uboat.net/wwi/ships_hit/875.html)
- The **SS Bencoolen**, a 1,455-ton, fully-rigged East Indiaman barque built 1855, wrecked on **Summerleaze Beach, Bude**, on 21 October 1862 while sailing from Liverpool to Bombay; she broke her main mast in a NNW gale, became unsteerable, and grounded; 27 of her 33 crew drowned. Her figurehead is preserved in Bude's town museum, and wire from the wreck was reportedly used to form a fence along the Strand at Bude. — [Search aggregation citing cornishmemory.com, Wikipedia "Bencoolen (ship)", and opc-cornwall.org inquest records]
- The brig **Caledonia** (c.200 tons, built in Arbroath), homeward bound from Odessa, wrecked on **Sharpnose Point, between Bude and Morwenstow**, on 7 September 1842; all crew but one drowned, and they are buried at Morwenstow churchyard beneath the ship's figurehead (a memorial erected by Rev. R. S. Hawker). — [Cornwall Heritage — Ertach Kernow blog](https://www.cornwallheritage.com/ertach-kernow-blogs-2020-2021/ertach-kernow-for-those-in-peril-cornwalls-north-coast-before-the-lifeboats/); [Wikipedia — Caledonia (1839 brig)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caledonia_(1839_brig))
- The Italian barque **Concezione** (420 tons, built San Mauro 1879) was driven ashore at **Widemouth Bay** (just south of Bude) by northwesterly gales in November 1900. — [Search aggregation, visitcornwall.tv-sourced local history snippet]
- Additional named Bude-area strandings with less detail: the **Raphael** (lost in Bude Bay, 1468, en route Danzig to Bristol); the **Fanny** (wrecked at Bude, 1772, all 3 crew lost); the **Alert**, a 177-ton slave-trade brig (wrecked at Bude, 1790, Bristol to Africa); the **Frieza**, a Norwegian brig that drifted ashore, overturned, under Efford Cliff at Bude; the **Ability** (from Cork for Penzance, wrecked near Bude 1824, crew lost); the **Giraffa** of Scarborough (oats cargo from Cork to London, driven ashore in Bude Bay in a NW gale, 1826). — [oceantreasures.org / search aggregation]; note: site could not be directly fetched (SSL certificate mismatch), so these are search-snippet sourced only, not independently verified against a primary record.
- Note "HMS Widemouth Bay" is a Bay-class Royal Navy frigate (launched 1944) *named after* Widemouth Bay — it is not a wreck located there and should not be confused with an actual Widemouth Bay shipwreck. — [Wikipedia — HMS Widemouth Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Widemouth_Bay)

### Inferences
- The "Shipwreck Coast" branding is justified by sheer historical volume (estimated 1,000 wrecks within 5 miles of this stretch, per the Visit Cornwall dive guide), but very few of these have individually surveyed, publicly documented GPS positions and dive depths — most are historical strandings known from newspaper/parish records rather than from diver surveys or UKHO wreck records with precise data.
- The only wreck on this specific stretch with concrete, verifiable physical remains confirmed as visible/accessible today is the **S89 E-boat at Tremoutha Haven near Crackington Haven** — but this is an intertidal/beach wreck (visible at low tide), not a scuba depth dive.
- Divers based in Bude most likely treat "diving this coast" as reef/scenic diving around headlands (Dizzard Point, Cambeak) with the chance of encountering unidentified/uncharted wreck debris, rather than diving a roster of named, charted wrecks — consistent with the vague "wreck sites marked on chart 1178" language in the dive guide, which stops short of naming specific dived wrecks.

### Gaps
- Could not verify precise GPS coordinates or UKHO/Wrecksite.eu record numbers for any named wreck in the Bude–Crackington Haven–Boscastle stretch specifically (wrecksite.eu was not directly accessible/searchable with this toolset; only secondary summaries were found).
- Could not confirm a charted diveable depth for any of the Bude/Widemouth/Boscastle-area historical wrecks (Bencoolen, Caledonia, Concezione, Jessie Logan, Gazelle, SS Boscastle) — these appear to be beach/rock strandings largely broken up or buried, not offshore dive sites with a known depth in metres.
- Could not independently verify the Raphael/Fanny/Alert/Frieza/Ability/Giraffa details against a primary source — the source (oceantreasures.org) could not be fetched directly due to an SSL hostname mismatch error, so these rest on a single secondary aggregation and should be treated as lower-confidence pending verification.
- Did not find a UK Diving (ukdiving.co.uk) wreck-database page specifically for this stretch — the wreck-list page could not be reached (connection refused during this research session).

## What are their depths and rough positions relative to named coastal landmarks (Dizzard Point, Cambeak, Hartland Point, etc.)?

### Takeaway
Landmark-relative positions are documented qualitatively (e.g., "between Bude and Morwenstow," "below Willapark," "near the lighthouse") for most historical wrecks, but almost none carry a stated depth in metres/feet, since these are coastal strandings rather than surveyed offshore dive sites.

### Cited Findings
- **S89 (E-boat)**: ashore at Tremoutha Haven, ~0.5 mile south of Crackington Haven beach; remains sit among boulders, intertidal (no diving depth applicable). — [Submerged.co.uk](https://www.submerged.co.uk/crackington-haven-s89/)
- **Caledonia**: wrecked at Sharpnose Point, positioned between Bude and Morwenstow. — [Cornwall Heritage](https://www.cornwallheritage.com/ertach-kernow-blogs-2020-2021/ertach-kernow-for-those-in-peril-cornwalls-north-coast-before-the-lifeboats/)
- **Bencoolen**: grounded on Summerleaze Beach, directly at Bude Haven. — [search aggregation, cornishmemory.com/Wikipedia]
- **Jessie Logan**: wrecked "below Willapark," the headland immediately north of Boscastle harbour. — [The Riverside, Boscastle](https://riversideboscastle.co.uk/boscastle-a-history-of-shipwrecks-and-wrecking/)
- **Concezione**: driven ashore at Widemouth Bay, just south of Bude. — [search aggregation]
- **MS Johanna** (modern, 31 Dec 1982): driven aground on rocks less than 400 m from Hartland Point lighthouse while carrying wheat from the Netherlands toward Cardiff up the Bristol Channel; decaying hull remains visible. Four crew winched off by RAF Chivenor helicopter, three officers later taken off by Clovelly RNLI lifeboat. — [Wikipedia — Hartland Point, via search aggregation]; photo documented at [Geograph](https://www.geograph.org.uk/photo/6442084)
- **SS Hoche** (French steamship): ran aground near Hartland Point in thick fog on 2 July 1882. — [search aggregation of Wikipedia/Hartland sources]
- **SS Rosalia**: Italian steam cargo ship that ran aground in dense fog at Hartland Quay on 27 May 1904, en route Genoa to Newport, Monmouthshire; "her iron bones can still be seen at low tide." — [search aggregation re: Hartland Quay Shipwreck Museum]
- **SS Ginetorix**: named alongside SS Rosalia as wrecked at Hartland Quay, but no further detail (date, cause, depth) was found in this research. — [search aggregation re: Hartland Quay Shipwreck Museum]
- A Historic England Heritage Gateway record exists for "Wreck of the Mousse Le Moyec off Hartland Point" (record MDV51976), but the page could not be fetched (HTTP 403) during this research, so no detail beyond the title/location is confirmed. — [Historic England Heritage Gateway listing page, URL only; content not verified]
- Dive-guide-level "dive area" landmarks cited generically (not tied to a specific named wreck): Dizzard Point and Cambeak, described only as having "plenty of shallow and deep wreck sites" without individual names, positions, or depths given. — [Visit Cornwall TV — Cornwall Divers Guide North Coast](https://www.visitcornwall.tv/cornwall-divers-guide-north-coast/)

### Inferences
- Of the landmark-tied wrecks found, the Hartland Point/Hartland Quay cluster (MS Johanna, SS Hoche, SS Rosalia, SS Ginetorix, Mousse Le Moyec) is the most densely documented sub-area, likely owing to the presence of the Hartland Quay Shipwreck and Smuggling Museum actively curating this local history.
- None of the wrecks found between Hartland Point and Boscastle have a confirmed scuba-diveable depth in metres; they read as shoreline/rock strandings, consistent with this coast's reputation for rocky headlands and shipwrecking rather than deep-water sinkings.

### Gaps
- No stated depth (in metres or feet) was found for any single named wreck between Hartland Point and Boscastle/Crackington Haven — this is a significant information gap for a diver-facing summary and would need direct queries to Wrecksite.eu, UKHO data, or a local BSAC club/chart to fill.
- The Historic England record for "Mousse Le Moyec off Hartland Point" could not be read (403 error) — worth a follow-up fetch attempt or a direct Historic England NRHE search by a researcher with browser access.
- No information found tying a specific named wreck directly to Dizzard Point or Cambeak by name — only generic "wreck sites" language from a tourism-oriented dive guide.

## What wrecks exist around Lundy Island that north Cornwall-based divers would reasonably travel to (via Ilfracombe or Bude-area charters)?

### Takeaway
Lundy Island has a well-documented, much richer set of named, depth-specific, charter-dived wrecks than the Bude-area mainland coast — including two legally Protected Wrecks (Iona II and HMS Montagu) — and is reached by dedicated charter boats from Ilfracombe (not from Bude directly), making it a plausible special-trip destination for a Bude-based club rather than a routine local dive.

### Cited Findings
- **MV Robert**: a 50 m coaster resting on her starboard side, lying 1 mile east of Lundy at 24 m depth; sank in 1975; described as "the only intact wreck to dive at Lundy." Marine life includes plumose anemones and bib fish. — [Lundy Diving — Dive page](https://www.lundydiving.co.uk/dive); [Calshot Divers — Diving the Robert at Lundy Island](https://www.calshotdivers.com/diving-the-robert-at-lundy-island)
- **Carmine Filomena**: Italian steam-powered cargo vessel carrying coal from South Wales to Genoa; ran aground off Rat Island (Lundy) in 1937; lies in 8–18 m; now partly kelp-covered. — [Lundy Diving — Dive page](https://www.lundydiving.co.uk/dive)
- **PS Iona II**: a Clyde-built (Govan, 1863) luxury paddle steamer, capable of 24 knots, purchased by Confederate agent Charles Hopkins Boster to run the Union blockade; sank east of Lundy on 2 February 1864 after taking on water en route from the Clyde to Kingston, Jamaica (via a Confederate transshipment route). Lies at approximately 25 m depth off Lundy's east coast; boilers and funnels are visible. Designated a Protected Wreck under the Protection of Wrecks Act 1973 on 6 December 1989; a licence is required to dive her, and an official Iona II Dive Trail has been established by Wessex Archaeology for licensed visiting divers. — [BSAC — Diving the Iona II](https://www.bsac.com/news-and-blog/diving-the-iona-ii-paddle-steamer-off-lundy-island/); [Lundy MPA — PS Iona II](https://www.lundympa.org.uk/shipwrecks/ps-iona-ii); [Wessex Archaeology — Iona II](https://www.wessexarch.co.uk/news/iona-ii); [Wikipedia — Iona II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iona_II)
- **HMS Montagu**: a Duncan-class pre-dreadnought Royal Navy battleship that ran at high speed into Shutter Rock on the southwest corner of Lundy at 02:00 on 30 May 1906 during radio-trial exercises in thick fog; suffered a 91-foot gash in her starboard hull and was declared a total wreck by August 1906 after salvage of her main guns. Lies in 4–15 m depth; extensive wreckage remains beneath kelp. Now a Scheduled Monument with legal protection (heritage protection confirmed 2019). — [Submerged.co.uk — HMS Montagu, Lundy Island](https://submerged.co.uk/hms-montagu-lundy-island/); [ITV News, 20 Sept 2019](https://www.itv.com/news/2019-09-20/heritage-protection-for-battleship-wreck-and-lundy-island-cliff-face-steps); [Lundy MPA — HMS Montagu](https://www.lundympa.org.uk/shipwrecks/hms-montagu)
- **Gull Rock Wreck**: an unidentified armed vessel, possibly a Genoese carrack, believed lost between the 15th and 16th centuries (possibly 1418) off Gull Rock, Lundy (51°11′07″N 4°39′24″W); lies at approximately 30 m; cargo includes wrought-iron breech blocks, a wrought-iron gun, and stone shot. Discovered 1968, rediscovered 1983; designated a Protected Wreck on 12 February 1990 and managed by Historic England; surveyed by the Archaeological Diving Unit (1989) and Wessex Archaeology (2004). — [Wikipedia — Gull Rock Wreck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gull_Rock_Wreck)
- Lundy's diving is seasonal — good visibility typically late April to end of September — and the island hosts "over 40 dive sites" combining wrecks, reefs, drop-offs and pinnacles, with grey seals a major draw alongside the wreck diving. — [Lundy Field Society — Diving](http://www.lundy.org.uk/about-lundy/diving); [Big Squid — Scuba Diving Lundy](https://www.bigsquid.co.uk/scuba-diving-travel/lundy/)
- Charter access to Lundy for diving is from **Ilfracombe**, not Bude: operators named include Lundy Diving (private charter, up to 12 divers per boat, 2 dives per day trip, dive lift fitted), Wild Frontier Charters, and Easy Divers North Devon (seal-encounter/dive trips). Trips run roughly 7am–6:30pm depending on tides, about 8 hours round-trip. — [Lundy Diving — Dive page](https://www.lundydiving.co.uk/dive); [Wild Frontier Charters — Lundy diving trips](https://wildfrontiercharters.co.uk/diving-trips-lundy-island-ilfracombe/); [Easy Divers North Devon](https://www.easydiversnorthdevon.co.uk/seal-encounter-dive-trips/)

### Inferences
- The Lundy wreck roster is far better documented (named vessels, specific depths, protection status) than the mainland Bude-to-Boscastle coast, likely because Lundy's wrecks lie in clearer, deeper water suited to recreational scuba and because two are legally Protected Wrecks with active archaeological/survey programmes (Wessex Archaeology, Historic England), generating public documentation.
- For a Bude-based BSAC club, Lundy is realistically reached as a full-day special trip via an Ilfracombe charter (roughly 1–1.5 hours' drive from Bude to Ilfracombe, then an ~8-hour boat day) rather than a boat launched directly from Bude — no operator or source found offering direct Bude-to-Lundy charter trips.
- Divers intending to visit Iona II or HMS Montagu specifically need to arrange/hold the required Protection of Wrecks Act licence in advance, which is an added planning step beyond a standard reef/wreck day trip.

### Gaps
- Could not find a published depth or further detail for the Gull Rock Wreck's "approximately 30 m" figure cross-checked against a second independent source; Wikipedia's "Gull Rock Wreck" article is the only direct source fetched and does not itself state a depth (the 30 m figure came from the Lundy Diving dive-list page, so this one single-source combination is flagged as moderate-confidence only).
- Could not confirm whether any Bude-based dive club/charter runs trips to Lundy directly (vs. requiring a separate trip via Ilfracombe) — no source found addressing this logistics question directly.
- Did not find additional named Lundy wrecks beyond the five above (MV Robert, Carmine Filomena, Iona II, HMS Montagu, Gull Rock Wreck) despite Lundy supposedly having "40+ dive sites [...] wrecks, reefs, drop offs, and pinnacles" — most of those 40+ sites are evidently reef/scenic sites, not wrecks, since no further wreck names surfaced across five different Lundy-diving-focused sources (Lundy Diving, Wild Frontier Charters, PADI, Big Squid, Lundy Field Society).

## Is there an Admiralty chart or wreck database (UKHO, Wrecksite.eu, Historic England NRHE) with entries for this stretch?

### Takeaway
Yes in principle — Admiralty Chart 1178 (Approaches to Bristol Channel) is repeatedly cited by local dive-guide sources as marking wreck sites along this coast, and Historic England's Heritage Gateway/NRHE holds at least one specific record for this stretch (the Mousse Le Moyec off Hartland Point) plus the Lundy Protected Wrecks — but this research could not directly access or query Wrecksite.eu or the UKHO's own wreck database to extract a name-by-name list for the Hartland Point–Boscastle stretch, and one Historic England record page returned a 403 error when fetched directly.

### Cited Findings
- **Admiralty Chart 1178** ("Approaches to Bristol Channel," 1:200,000 scale) is cited by a north-Cornwall diving guide as the chart on which wreck sites around Dizzard Point/Cambeak and the Bude area are marked. — [Visit Cornwall TV — Cornwall Divers Guide North Coast](https://www.visitcornwall.tv/cornwall-divers-guide-north-coast/)
- **Wrecksite.eu** is described (via Wikipedia) as the world's largest online nautical wreck database, with over 200,000 wreck entries and 180,000+ positions; it is a plausible/likely source for detailed entries on this coast, but this research was not able to directly search or fetch wrecksite.eu content (only secondary/aggregated mentions were retrieved, and a 2019 diving-forum thread noted some users have found the site's full records require registration/payment). — [Wikipedia — Wrecksite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrecksite); [The Dive Forum — "Anyone got access to Wrecksite.eu???"](https://www.thediveforum.com/forum/general-diving-forums/wreck-diving/wreck-research-location-and-exploration/1630-anyone-got-access-to-wrecksite-eu)
- **Historic England / Heritage Gateway (NRHE)** holds a record titled "Wreck of the Mousse Le Moyec off Hartland Point" (record MDV51976), confirming Historic England does catalogue wrecks specifically in this stretch — but the page's content (date, depth, cause) could not be retrieved in this research due to a 403 Forbidden error on direct fetch. — [Historic England Heritage Gateway, record MDV51976](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/heritage-gateway/results/record/c39bc47e-3b72-4a06-9863-681629e456a2/MDV51976) (title/URL only confirmed; content unverified)
- Historic England also formally lists and protects the **Gull Rock Wreck** (list entry, Protected Wreck designated 1990) and **PS Iona II** (list entry 1000051, designated 1989) at Lundy, and in 2019 granted heritage protection (Scheduled Monument status) to **HMS Montagu** at Lundy. — [Historic England — Iona II list entry 1000051](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1000051); [ITV News, 20 Sept 2019, on HMS Montagu protection](https://www.itv.com/news/2019-09-20/heritage-protection-for-battleship-wreck-and-lundy-island-cliff-face-steps)
- **UK Diving (ukdiving.co.uk)** maintains a general wreck-list database page, suggested as a resource for UK wreck diving including Cornwall, but this research could not connect to the site (connection refused) to confirm whether it holds specific entries for the Hartland Point–Boscastle stretch. — [UK Diving wreck list page, URL only; content not verified: http://www.ukdiving.co.uk/wrecks/wreck_list.php]

### Inferences
- A proper database-level answer (full name/position/depth list for every charted wreck between Hartland Point and Boscastle) almost certainly exists within Wrecksite.eu and/or UKHO's hydrographic wreck records, since Admiralty Chart 1178 is explicitly said to mark these wreck sites — but extracting it requires direct, likely registered or paid, access to those databases, which this research's available tools (general web search/fetch) could not achieve.
- Historic England's Heritage Gateway/NRHE is confirmed to hold at least granular, named records for this stretch (demonstrated by the Mousse Le Moyec record), suggesting a systematic NRHE search (e.g., via the Heritage Gateway's own search interface rather than a direct deep link, which triggered a 403) would likely surface further named wrecks for Hartland Point through Boscastle that this research did not uncover through general web search.

### Gaps
- Could not directly query or browse Wrecksite.eu, UKHO's wreck database, or UK Diving's wreck list for a specific, exhaustive list of named/charted wrecks in the Hartland Point–Crackington Haven/Boscastle stretch — this is the single largest gap in this research and the most promising next step for a follow-up researcher with direct site access (the Heritage Gateway/NRHE search interface, reached via its homepage rather than a direct record-permalink, is also worth retrying, since the one direct record link attempted returned a 403).
- Could not retrieve the content of the one confirmed Historic England record found for this stretch (Mousse Le Moyec, off Hartland Point) due to a 403 error — a retry via a different access method (e.g., a cached version, or the Heritage Gateway's own search tool rather than the direct record URL) is recommended.
